PoseSplit: A Synthetic-to-Real Audio-Plus-Pose Pilot for Camera Reframing

Camera reframing and microphone reorientation change different relationships between a scene and its sensors. We report a frozen CPU pilot that separates these operations in a small synthetic-to-real spatial task. Five seeds produce 1,600 four-channel waveforms: 1,280 for training and 320 for validation. Fifteen small predictors use acoustic intensity, coherence and known relative yaw to classify geometric field-of-view membership. Evaluation uses 168 half-second windows from 14 real STARSS23 recordings in seven held-out rooms, with four fixed headings. Under camera-only reframing, mean balanced accuracy is .829 for pose-aware training, .480 for canonical-orientation training, .408 for intentionally incorrect pose labels and .782 for analytic geometry. Under virtual co-rig reorientation, canonical training instead reaches .841 versus .832 for pose-aware training. Thus pose-aware training does not dominate both regimes. The analytic direction estimate has median error 23.48 degrees, compared with 50.14 for a constant-front reference; this is a separate measurement from the learned binary task. Independent raw-data replay verifies the reported observations. The results support a bounded audio-plus-known-pose data interface and real-room feasibility. They do not demonstrate visual grounding, realistic neural audio generation, unseen-angle generalization, or the proposed development-driven coverage sampler.
